## Objective

Turn the parsed document into retrieval units that are safe to cite: units that
never straddle a scope boundary, carry every filter key a search needs, point at a
stored grid when they came from a table, and have identities stable enough for a
citation to survive a reindex.

## Why now

Slice 1.4 defined what a `CorpusChunk` is; nothing produces one. Slice 1.6's
retrieval service and V3.3's `search_company_corpus` tool both need something to
return.

## The chunking strategy, and why it is not a sliding window

A retrieval unit has one job: be the thing you put in front of a reader as "here
is where this came from". That rules out both obvious answers.

*A fixed-width sliding window* cuts a sentence in half and, worse, cuts across a
heading. A window starting in "Group results" and ending in "Jewellery Maisons"
carries two scopes and can be cited as either — a scope error baked into the
retrieval unit itself, which is precisely what the period/scope apparatus exists
to prevent.

*A whole section* can run for twenty pages of an annual report. Retrieving it
answers "which section" when the question was "which sentence".

So the strategy is structural first, size second:

1. **Never cross a section boundary.** The hard rule. A section is where the
   extractor's own heading structure says the subject — and the scope — changed.
2. **Prefer a page boundary**, once there is at least `min_chars` worth citing on
   this side of it. A chunk on one page cites as "page 14"; one straddling two
   cites as "pages 14-15", which is honest but less useful.
3. **Then a paragraph boundary**, never mid-sentence.
4. **`max_chars` last**, and only for a single paragraph longer than the bound —
   which bounds a pathological document rather than expressing a policy.
5. **Tables are never merged into prose.**

### Why there is no overlap

Sliding-window chunkers overlap because fixed-width splitting cuts through
meaning; overlap is the patch. This chunker splits at paragraph boundaries inside
a section, so the patch is unnecessary — and it would cost something real. Two
chunks containing the same sentence produce two hits for one piece of evidence and
a citation that could name either. A test asserts no overlap setting exists, and
another asserts the spans of a split section are exactly contiguous: no gap
(evidence would be unreachable) and no overlap.

### A table chunk points at the grid; it does not replace it

`DATA_AND_EVIDENCE_ARCHITECTURE` §2.2 says tables are not chunks — a borderless
five-year summary must survive as a grid, because its column→year map is the whole
reason the geometric reconstructor exists. It must also be *findable*, or an agent
cannot reach it.

So both. `ResearchDocumentTable` stays authoritative; the table chunk is a
searchable surface carrying `research_document_table_id`, so a hit resolves to the
grid rather than to the flattened text it was found by. The rendering leads with
the column periods, because "revenue 32,549" is not evidence and "FY2025 revenue
32,549" is, and a hit is often read without opening the grid behind it.

### Chunk identity is derived, not assigned

```
sha256(version_id | pipeline_version | kind | ordinal | char_start | char_end | table_location)
```

A row id belongs to whichever database wrote it, so rebuilding the corpus would
invalidate every citation. Re-running the same parser reproduces the same ids
exactly, so reindexing puts the same span back in the same slot. Re-parsing under
a **new** pipeline version deliberately produces new ids — it is a different
reading of the document — and the old derivation and its chunks are retained, so
the old citation still resolves too.

## Why chunks are database rows and not only index entries

Chunks are corpus **data**. They live in PostgreSQL beside the pages they came
from, carry the citation lineage, and exist whether or not a search backend has
ever been configured.

That is what makes [OPEN DECISION #1](../OPEN_DECISIONS.md#1-azure-ai-search-vs-postgresql--pgvector)
cheap to defer *and* cheap to change: whichever backend is chosen, switching to it
means reindexing from rows that are already there rather than re-fetching and
re-parsing every document in the corpus.

## Security considerations

- **Governance is stamped at persistence, not checked at query time.**
  `indexable` is computed once from the version's access class and stored on the
  chunk, so the constraint travels with the data into every backend. A policy
  check that only happens on the way out is one that whichever read path forgets
  it will skip. A `licensed_private` document's chunks are stored, citable and
  never indexed — asserted by test.
- Company scoping is a stored column, so entity isolation is a query constraint
  rather than a presentation choice.
- `index_version` refuses to index a version with no **active** derivation:
  indexing a superseded one would put a stale reading of the document in front of
  a researcher.

## Non-goals

- Embeddings. `CorpusChunk.embedding` is the slot; who fills it is a provider
  decision in V3.4.
- Any production search backend (OPEN DECISION #1).
- The typed retrieval service (Slice 1.6) — `index_version` takes a backend from
  its caller and resolves nothing from configuration, because there is nothing to
  resolve yet.
- Transcript speaker turns and presentation slides. The chunker is structural, so
  they are a new source of section boundaries rather than a new strategy; they
  arrive with V3.4.8's transcript provider.
