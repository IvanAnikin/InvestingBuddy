# V3.11 Slice 11.2 — real-document scope resolution

## The problem

V3.10 measured the real Richemont FY26 annual report at **170 of 173 chunks with
`scope_key = NULL`**, and audited the three that were labelled:

| Label | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `group` | defensible |
| `group` | defensible |
| `segment:proposed dividend` | **wrong — a dividend is not a business segment** |

So the platform's central promise — a segment figure is never presented as a Group figure —
was being kept by *declining to label almost everything*. Safe, and not correct. It also
capped usefulness: scope-filtered retrieval over a real annual report returned nearly
nothing.

**One cause.** The only scope signal was `infer_heading_scope`, which fires when a heading
contains generic reporting vocabulary — "segment", "by region", "consolidated". Real
annual reports name their segments: "Jewellery Maisons", "Specialist Watchmakers". Those
headings contain none of that vocabulary, so they produced nothing. And the fallback rule
— *any leaf heading under a segment-ish ancestor is a segment* — is what manufactured
`segment:proposed dividend`.

## The insight

**A report tells you its own segment names.** It publishes them in a segment note, a
"sales by business area" chart, or a segment table. The vocabulary does not have to be
hardcoded per issuer — which would be an issuer-specific hack, and is forbidden — it can
be **learned from the document being parsed** and applied to the rest of that same
document.

On the real report, from the real highlights page, with no issuer knowledge:

```
segments learned  3: Jewellery Maisons, Specialist Watchmakers, Other Businesses
  'Specialist Watchmakers' <- unit-labelled item in a 'by business area' disclosure
```

That is the layer that turns "Specialist Watchmakers" from an unrecognised string into a
known reporting segment of this issuer, in this document, for this period.

## The layers

Ordered most-authoritative first, stopping at the first that decides:

| # | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `explicit` | structured extractor metadata |
| 2 | `table_caption` | the table's own caption |
| 3 | `heading` | section heading ancestry, **now vetted against the vocabulary** |
| 4 | `nearby_heading` | the leaf heading, adjacent chunk only, corroborated |
| 5 | `row_label` | a table whose row labels are segment names |
| 6 | `vocabulary` | the document's own learned names, found in the text |
| 7 | `classifier` | bounded model, **off by default, cannot answer "group"** |
| 8 | `unknown` | no signal, or conflicting signals |

Every result carries `method`, `confidence`, `evidence` and — when it refused — an
`ambiguity` reason from a closed set.

## The safety property

> **No layer may promote a segment figure to Group scope.**

Enforced three ways rather than by care:

1. **A chunk naming more than one scope resolves to UNKNOWN.** The real highlights page
   carries three segment figures *and* the Group total; it cannot be cited as either.
2. **`group_claim_signal` has two strengths, and the asymmetry is the point.**
   *Entity* (liberal) detects conflict — being liberal pushes toward unknown.
   *Measure* (conservative) is required to assert Group from prose — being conservative
   also pushes toward unknown. Both fail closed.
3. **The classifier's return type has no representation for Group.** A model may refine a
   segment attribution and is structurally unable to promote anything.

## What the real document caught

The first draft scored 22.5% coverage. An audit of every label found it had bought that
number with wrong answers, and four fixes followed — each one a real defect:

| Defect | Fix |
|---|---|
| A paragraph about a Maison's creative director labelled `group` because it mentioned "the Group" in passing | Group assertion from prose requires a **measure**, not a mention |
| `"At Group level, operating profit came in at € 4.5 billion"` **escaped the conflict guard** — the third-party-company rule read sentence-initial "At Group" as a corporate name and deleted the signal | Function words excluded from the third-party rule |
| `"consolidated financial statements"` asserted Group — it names a *document*, not a figure | Removed from the measure pattern |
| Central-functions prose inherited a segment from a heading several chunks away | `nearby_heading` requires adjacency **and** corroboration |

Coverage settled at **8.1%**, and that is the honest number: the tightening was worth more
than the coverage it cost.

## Measured result — real Richemont FY26 annual report

```
chunks 173     segments learned: Jewellery Maisons, Specialist Watchmakers, Other Businesses

                    before     after   delta
group                    2         9      +7
segment                  1         5      +4
unknown                170       159     -11
scope coverage        1.7%      8.1%   +6.4%

why a chunk stayed unknown
  no_scope_signal                  140
  segment_and_group_in_one_chunk    12     ← refused, with a reason
  multiple_segments_in_one_chunk     7     ← refused, with a reason

SAFETY
  group-labelled chunks             9
  ...that name a reporting segment  0
  false-positive Group rate      0.0%
```

Coverage is **4.8× better**, `segment:proposed dividend` is gone, 19 chunks now refuse
*with a documented reason* instead of silently having no signal, and **no segment figure
was promoted to Group**.

The remaining 140 unscoped chunks are overwhelmingly narrative with no figure in them, on
a two-column PDF whose heading/column interleaving is a known upstream limitation. Scope
is a property of a figure; prose without one does not need it.

## The mandatory regression

> *A Specialist Watchmakers figure must never be promoted to Group scope.*

The real corpus contains the trap, and the **€ 107 million** figure is in the same chunk:

> *"**The Group's** Specialist Watchmakers reported sales of € 3.1 billion… The operating
> result came in at **€ 107 million**… On 22 January 2026, Richemont and **the Damiani
> Group**, a prestigious, family-run Italian global **luxury group**…"*

One chunk, four traps: the possessive "The Group's", a third party's corporate name twice,
and a lowercase common-noun "group". It resolves to **`segment:specialist watchmakers`** —
outcome **A** of the acceptance brief's two permitted outcomes.

`tests/test_v3_scope_resolution.py` — **44 tests**, all built from real structural
patterns, none keyed off the issuer's name.

## The measurement tool

`scripts/v3-scope-acceptance.py` rebuilds the parse from what the corpus already persisted,
re-resolves every chunk, and **exits non-zero on any unsafe Group promotion**:

```
--expect "operating result came in at € 107 million=segment:specialist watchmakers"
--expect "The Group’s Specialist Watchmakers reported sales=segment:specialist watchmakers?"
--forbid-group "Specialist Watchmakers"
```

The `?` suffix encodes the brief's own standard: *resolve correctly, or stay explicitly
unknown — never Group*. One of the two chunks holding that sentence also carries the
Group's € 3.5 billion profit and its net cash position, so it legitimately takes the
second outcome.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Scope coverage materially improved on a real document. ✅ 1.7% → 8.1%.
2. Zero unsafe Group promotion. ✅ false-positive Group rate 0.0%.
3. The Specialist Watchmakers figure resolves to its segment and never to Group. ✅
4. Jewellery Maisons remains a distinct series; Group figures remain Group. ✅
5. Unknown remains unknown, with a reason. ✅ 19 refusals now carry one.
6. No issuer-specific rule anywhere. ✅ the vocabulary is learned per document.
