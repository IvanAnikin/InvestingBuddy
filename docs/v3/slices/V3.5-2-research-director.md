# V3.5 Slice 5.2 — the Research Director

## Objective

Bounded planning: subject → playbook → prior research → prioritized questions →
role assignments → budget, written to the ledger — with every question no role can answer
becoming a **gap** rather than an assignment.

## Why now

5.1 built the ledger and nothing writes to it. The Director is its first writer, and 5.3's
loop consumes what the Director produces.

## Current implementation

V2 has no planner. The council receives a frozen evidence pack assembled up front
(`evidence_pack.py`, 1,047 lines) and reasons over exactly that. Roles are a fixed
sequence in `llm/schemas.py:53-62` with their behaviour in prompts.

## Proposed changes

Two modules: roles **as data**, and a planner.

### Roles are records, not prompts

Every role declares its tools, source classes, iteration cap, tool budget and what it does
when evidence is missing. A prompt is invisible, untestable and unversioned; a declaration
can be diffed, reviewed and asserted against. Three constructor refusals make the
declaration load-bearing:

- a role with **no tools** cannot be declared — that is a gap generator, not an
  investigator;
- a role naming a **tool that does not exist** cannot be declared, because it would
  silently be unable to answer anything and the failure would look like a research gap;
- `on_missing_evidence` may only be `raise_gap`. **"Assert anyway" is not expressible.**

### The mechanism

> **A role with no declared tool for a question cannot answer it. It raises a gap.**

`roles_that_can_answer(required_tools)` is a set operation, so "nobody can answer this" is
a *fact* the Director acts on rather than a judgement a model makes mid-run. It persists as
a `tool_unavailable` gap with `closable=False`, and if the question was blocking, the gap
blocks the Council.

### The deterministic core is the product

Planning is composition: playbook questions first (they are the methodology), then prior
open gaps, then a short baseline. A model may **refine** — reorder, add at most three —
and it can do none of the following:

| A model cannot | Why |
|---|---|
| Delete a question by omitting it from its ordering | Anything omitted is appended. A model must not be able to drop a playbook's mandatory question by leaving it out of a list. |
| Mark a question blocking | A blocking question stops the Council convening, so a model that could set one could stop every run. **Only a playbook may.** |
| Exceed the addition budget | Three, counted. |
| Take the plan down | A refusal or an exception returns the plan **unchanged**, with `refined_by_model=False`. |

Every added question carries `origin=director`, so "the model added this" is queryable.

### Bounded, and the bounds are not its own

`max_tasks`, `max_rounds` and `max_tool_calls` come from `ModeLimits` (4.11). The Director
records them; it does not choose them. `ABSOLUTE_MAX_QUESTIONS` is a ceiling no mode can
raise — a plan with 400 questions is not a plan. A question dropped for capacity is
recorded as a `budget_exhausted` gap, never silently absent, because a plan that quietly
forgot a playbook question would report a methodology it did not apply. **A blocking
question is never dropped**: it sorts ahead of everything.

## Security considerations

- The plan type **cannot express "research everything about this company"**: questions and
  tasks are both capped, and the caps come from the mode rather than from the plan.
- A model's contribution is bounded in count, attributed by origin, and cannot set the one
  flag that would stop the platform working.
- `PlaybookLike` is a Protocol, so the Director imports no playbook implementation and
  cannot be made to execute one's code.

## Unit tests

Roles: every declared tool is real; the three refusals; every role raises a gap on missing
evidence; coverage as a set operation; the valuation role stating what it does not do.
Planning: baseline without a playbook; playbook questions first; an unanswerable question
becoming unassignable and *not* assigned anyway; a playbook specialist instantiated; prior
gaps carrying their origin; determinism across two identical runs; deeper modes permitting
more; the absolute cap; **a blocking question never dropped for capacity**. Refinement: a
model failure leaving the plan intact, a model unable to delete by omission, an addition
attributed and never blocking, the addition budget enforced. Persistence: the plan landing
in the ledger, an unassignable question becoming a Council-blocking gap, a dropped question
becoming a closable one.

## Integration tests

None new. The ledger's schema guarantees were exercised against real PostgreSQL in 5.1.

## Live issuer acceptance

Not performed. Nothing calls the Director on a live path; 5.3 is its first caller and it
too is off by default.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A question no role holds the tools for is **never assigned**, and becomes a
   `tool_unavailable` gap that blocks the Council when the question was blocking.
2. A model refiner cannot delete a question, cannot mark one blocking, cannot exceed its
   addition budget, and cannot take the plan down.
3. Two runs of the same inputs produce an identical plan.

## Non-goals

- Executing the plan. That is 5.3.
- Real playbooks. `PlaybookLike` is satisfied by V3.6's implementations; the Director must
  not be blocked on them, and a Director that could only run *with* a playbook could not
  research a company no playbook covers.
- Reading prior research itself. `prior_open_gaps` is passed in, which keeps memory from
  becoming a hidden input and keeps the Director testable.

## Rollback

Delete both modules. Nothing imports them at runtime.
