# V3.6 Slice 6.2 — the five industry playbooks

## Objective

Five materially different methodologies, and the demonstration that they *are* different.

## Why now

6.1 shipped a schema with no content, so every run still gets the same methodology. The
phase's acceptance criterion needs at least two playbooks to be checkable at all —
*"biotech and luxury runs of the same shape produce demonstrably different questions,
metrics and sources"* — which is why all five ship together rather than one per slice. They
are configuration against a schema already accepted, and the slice's own test is the
differentiation across the set.

## Current implementation

`app/services/playbooks/schema.py` and `registry.py` (6.1). No playbook exists.

## Proposed changes

`industries.py`: five `Playbook` declarations, registered at package import.

### Why these five

Each breaks the generic methodology in a **different** way, and each has a live company in
the regression set:

| Playbook | What generic analysis gets wrong | Regression issuer |
|---|---|---|
| `luxury` | Segment figures read as Group. | CFR |
| `biotech` | Revenue growth and margins on a company with no revenue. | MRNA |
| `semiconductors` | A cycle and a policy story read as a growth story. | ASML |
| `banks_financials` | A generic P&L reading, which produces **confident nonsense**. | *(to be added)* |
| `industrial_defense` | Demand announced years before it is revenue. | *(to be added)* |

### The absences are the methodology

`biotech.required_metrics` contains **no margin and no revenue**.
`banks_financials.required_metrics` contains **none of** `gross_margin`,
`operating_margin`, `capex_intensity`, `net_debt_to_ebitda` or `fcf_conversion` — for a
bank those are not merely unhelpful, they are meaningless, and reporting one lends a number
the authority of a metric. Both absences are asserted by tests, because an absence nobody
tests is an omission waiting to be "fixed".

### Blocking is used sparingly

One or two per playbook: the questions where proceeding without an answer produces an
analysis of the *wrong thing* rather than a thinner analysis of the right one. A test caps
it at two — more than that is a methodology that mostly refuses to report.

### The defect the playbooks exposed

Biotech's blocking `pipeline_state` requires `get_recent_filings`. Two roles **declare**
that tool and **nothing implements it** — it is one of the twelve names V3.3 reserved.
`RoleSpec` validates against the vocabulary, which is correct (a role is a declaration),
but the Director was assigning questions on the strength of it.

Fixed here: `plan_research` now checks required tools against the **registered** tools and
refuses at plan time with a distinct reason. The two reasons are kept apart because they
are different problems — `no implementation for [...]` is a platform gap;
`no role holds [...]` is a staffing one.

That failure would otherwise have surfaced as a mysterious tool refusal in the middle of a
run, and a mid-run refusal looks like a coverage problem where a plan-time one *is* a
coverage fact.

## Security considerations

A playbook selects from vocabularies that already exist and cannot widen what an agent may
do. `preferred_sources` naming a connector that does not exist produces an honest gap, not
a fetch: the entry is a *retrieval priority*, not a fetch authority.

## Unit tests

Differentiation: no two playbooks share a question key; biotech and luxury are disjoint;
biotech requires no margin; a bank requires none of the generic industrial metrics; luxury
makes segment scope blocking with the "not a Group figure" instruction in the question
text; distinct and barely-overlapping risk frameworks; sources and specialists differing by
industry. Declarations: every calculation, role and rule real; at least one blocking
question each and at most two; every playbook explaining itself. Selection: each sector
selecting its playbook; a `pre_revenue` signal selecting biotech with no sector; an
unclassifiable company getting none with a reason; a **conglomerate** getting both and
being harder to complete (ADR-054). Through the Director: biotech and luxury producing
different plans; a playbook instantiating its specialists; an **unanswerable blocking
question stopping the Council** and an answerable one not.

## Integration tests

None. No schema change.

## Live issuer acceptance

Not performed. A live run needs an investigator implementation, which does not exist.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Biotech and luxury runs of the same shape produce demonstrably different questions,
   metrics and sources.
2. A blocking question that cannot be answered prevents the Council from convening.
3. Every playbook's declarations are real, and every absence that carries meaning is
   asserted.

## Non-goals

- Sector-specific connectors. ClinicalTrials.gov, openFDA, central-bank statistics, NATO
  and SIPRI are named as `preferred_sources` and are not wired. One source wired correctly
  beats four half-wired ones — the lesson the 28 reference-only connectors already taught.
- A bank and a defence issuer in the regression set. Both are named in the acceptance
  strategy as *(later)* and adding a live issuer is not this slice's work.
- Playbook selection from a live classifier. `sector`/`industry` are the caller's input.

## Rollback

Delete `industries.py` and the `register_all()` call. The schema and registry stand alone.

## Dependencies

V3.6.1 (the schema), V3.5.2 (the Director), V3.3.3 (the calculations they require).
