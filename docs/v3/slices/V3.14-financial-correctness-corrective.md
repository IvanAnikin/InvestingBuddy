# V3.14 — Financial Correctness Corrective

**Branch** `fix/financial-correctness-corrective` · **PR** #195 · **No migration** (Alembic stays 038)

The first live production V3 report contained material financial errors. This slice
corrects them at their producers.

---

## The $19.263bn error

Five council sections asserted *"FY2025 revenue was $19.263 billion with an operating loss
of $3.074 billion and net loss of $2.822 billion."* The two losses are correct FY2025
figures. The revenue is **FY2022** — the COVID peak — presented as FY2025. Moderna's
actual FY2025 revenue is **$1.944bn**, so the report overstated it tenfold and then
reasoned about a decline from the wrong base.

Proven against Moderna's real SEC companyfacts:

| us-gaap concept | latest annual entry | value |
|---|---|---|
| `Revenues` | 2022-12-31 | **19.263bn** |
| `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` | 2025-12-31 | **1.944bn** |
| `OperatingIncomeLoss` | 2025-12-31 | -3.074bn |
| `NetIncomeLoss` | 2025-12-31 | -2.822bn |

Two defects compounding:

1. `parse_company_facts` walks its alias list in order and takes the **first concept with
   any annual data**, comparing periods only *within* that concept. Moderna stopped
   tagging `Revenues` after FY2022, so the stale tag won permanently.
2. `sec_edgar_fundamentals` merged the period-aware normalizer *underneath* the legacy
   parser — *"existing field_names from parse_company_facts win to preserve behavior."*
   `_select_metric` was written for exactly this bug, names Apple's stale `Revenues` tag
   in its docstring, gets Moderna right, and had its answer discarded. **Preserving that
   behaviour meant preserving the defect.**

The normalizer now wins, emits the base statement items rather than assuming the legacy
parser supplies them, and gives each fact its **own period end** as `as_of` instead of the
filing date — a three-year-old figure looked current because the *filing* was current.

## Period integrity

The report asserted *"Q2 2026 revenue compared to FY2025 annual revenue indicates a
continuing revenue decline trend"* and repeated it across the Red Team, the bear case and
the chair. Q2 2026 revenue was $145m; **Q2 2025 was $142m — a rise.** The decline came
entirely from setting one quarter against a full year, which the report's own snapshot
forbids in as many words: *"never comparable with each other and never annualised."*

The prohibition was a model instruction, which is not a control. And the sentence quotes
**no figures**, so every numeric check skipped it — the claim was invalid on its periods
alone. A deterministic guard now runs before the numeric gates and refuses a comparative
claim spanning different period **frequencies**.

Frequency, not `ReportingPeriod.comparable_with`: that method answers a stricter question
and refuses Q1 against Q2, and quarter-on-quarter is a real comparison. This codebase
withheld 32 correct segment sentences once by being clever with a threshold.

## Numeric parsing

Five real SEC-sourced claims were refused with *"has no numeric reading at all"* —
including `$1.0 billion` and `$(1.1) billion`. The parser stripped a trailing alpha run of
at most four characters, so `3.2bn` survived and `1.0 billion` did not.

Scale words and all three negative notations now parse. **No lead was forced to promote:**
all five still correctly fail verification, now with a truthful reason instead of a false
claim about unreadability.

## Reader-facing consistency

* **Latest annual said "Not reported" beside FY2025 claims.** Channel priority (issuer
  document > SEC XBRL) was applied without regard to period, so a single *interim* fact
  suppressed a complete regulator-published annual statement set.
* **Duplicate SEC events.** The data was correct — `recent_events` and `sec_filing_events`
  are two views, and the report's note says *"Do not add the two axes together."* The panel
  concatenated them. Now keyed on the accession-bearing URL, never headline text.
* **Repetition.** 68 claims held 22 redundant copies. Bear case 30 → 20 points.
* **Chair.** Traced in code: **expected, not a routing defect.** The council refused with
  `no_findings` while a chair model was routed and reachable. Both the degraded message and
  the panel said "no model available" — untrue. Now two named states.
* **Telemetry.** `DeepSeekModelProvider` had **no `consume_usage` at all**, so 76,853
  tokens were unattributable; an absent method silently means "spent nothing".
* **Source hierarchy.** A regulator source outranks a secondary one for the same fact; the
  secondary is kept as labelled corroboration.

## Independent review — seven substantive findings, all fixed

Two would have shipped defects worse than the ones being corrected:

1. **A claim and its negation were merged, and the negation deleted.** `"not"`, `"but"`
   and `"while"` were claim stopwords, so *"results support approval"* and *"results do
   **not** support approval"* reduced to identical word sets.
2. **The stale value could still ship silently.** A 10-K entry tagged `fp="Q4"` was
   neither annual nor quarterly, bypassing the period-aware selector entirely — shipping
   the FY2022 figure with no supersede warning.

Also fixed: scaled claims were near-unfalsifiable (any `1.0` anywhere verified
`$1.0 billion`); substring matching flagged *fellow*, *arose*, *dropout* and *growth*; the
guard refused period pairs a sentence never compared; claim identity collapsed every
blank-value lead onto one key; and the search leg's tokens were **costed at zero**.

The double-count question was settled: **no** — the routing slot is a
`DeepSeekModelProvider` and the search leg a `DeepSeekResearchProvider`, different classes
with different transports.
