# V3.7 Slice 7.1 — Council V2 inputs

## Objective

The Council reasons over the **ledger**, cites `finding_id`s that resolve, and **can be
refused**.

## Why now

V3.5 built the ledger and V3.6 the methodologies that fill it. Nothing consumes either.
Without this slice "Council V2" would be the same council with new prompts — the failure
the dependency order was written to avoid.

## Current implementation

`evidence_pack.py` (1,047 lines) assembles one frozen pack; `council.py` runs eight agents
over it; citations are positional run-local `E1`/`E2` handles that `citation_checker.py`
validates *within* the run and that nothing can resolve afterwards.

## Proposed changes

`app/services/council_v2/inputs.py`: `assemble(session, run) -> CouncilInput`.

### What deliberately does not change

The five allowed chair labels, the `implications` + chair `synthesis` contract ADR-041
established, and the rule that BUY/SELL/HOLD/WATCH are **absent from the type** rather than
filtered from the text. Those took several live corrections to get right, and a test in
this slice asserts the vocabulary is unchanged — V3.7 is not an excuse to reopen a safety
vocabulary.

### The one structural change

> A `key_point` references a **`finding_id`** that carries its own evidence, instead of a
> positional `E1`.

`E1` means "the first item in the pack this run happened to build". It cannot be resolved
next quarter, cannot be compared across runs, and is the reason research memory (V3.8) had
nothing stable to point at. `unresolvable_citations` makes the replacement checkable: a
`finding_id` either is in this run's ledger or is a fabrication.

### The Council can be refused

Three closed reasons: a blocking question open, a gap that blocks it, or **no
evidence-linked findings at all**. V2's council always ran, because nothing could say no.
When refused, the gaps still travel — the reader is told *why*.

### Conflict is an input

Unresolved disagreements are assembled and ordered first, because the Chair's job is to
**surface** them and never to silently pick a number. A Chair handed a tidy pack could not
do that: the conflict would already have been resolved by whoever built the pack.

### Two smaller properties that matter

- A **withdrawn** finding — one the Red Team retired — does not come back through the
  Council's front door.
- **Truncation is counted, not implied.** Each query fetches one row past its cap, which
  detects truncation cheaply and cannot count it; so when truncation is detected the real
  total is counted. `{"findings": 1}` when five were dropped tells a reader the pack is
  nearly complete when it is not.

## Security considerations

`CouncilInput` has **no free-text context field**, and a test asserts it: a Council that
could be handed unattributed prose would reintroduce exactly the failure the ledger
removed. Everything it carries came from a row that already had evidence behind it.

## Unit tests

Refusal on each of the three reasons, the closed vocabulary, and a reason beside a convened
council being unconstructible. Convening: playbook versions travelling; a withdrawn finding
excluded; verified findings sorting first so truncation drops the least supported material;
truncation counted rather than implied. Conflict: unresolved disagreements reaching the
Council, a resolved one carrying its explanation, gaps as an input. Citations: the
`finding_id` as the handle, an unresolvable citation detected, every finding carrying its
own evidence. Plus the safety-vocabulary guard.

## Integration tests

None new. Every read is over the ledger, whose guarantees were exercised against real
PostgreSQL in 5.1.

## Live issuer acceptance

Not performed. Nothing runs a council on this input yet.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A blocking question or a Council-blocking gap refuses the Council, with a reason from a
   closed vocabulary, and the gaps still travel.
2. Unresolved disagreements reach the Council intact.
3. A citation naming anything outside the run's findings is detectable.

## Non-goals

- Running the council. Wiring `council.py` to this input is a behaviour change on a live
  path and gets its own slice with its own acceptance.
- Changing any chair label, implication direction or fundamental setup.
- `ResearchDelta`. V3.8 adds it as a further Council input.

## Rollback

Delete the module. Nothing imports it at runtime.

## Dependencies

V3.5.1 (the ledger), V3.5.3 (what fills it), V3.6 (the playbook versions it pins).
