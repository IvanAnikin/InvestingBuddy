# V3.7 Slice 7.2 — the bounded Red Team challenge round

## Objective

A challenge that targets a `finding_id`, a response that must carry evidence, and an
unresolved challenge that **reaches the Chair intact**.

## Why now

7.1 gave the Council a ledger to read. The Red Team is the one Council role whose V2 form
is structurally unable to do its job: it can say a claim looks weak, and nothing can
answer.

## Current implementation

`red_team` is one agent in a fixed sequence (`llm/schemas.py:50`), reasoning over the same
frozen pack as everyone else. There is no mechanism for the challenged analyst to respond
and no record of what happened next.

## Proposed changes

`research_challenges` (migration 036) and `app/services/council_v2/red_team.py`.

### Exactly one round, enforced by the database

Multi-round debate between language models produces text, not truth: agents converge on
whoever wrote last and the token cost grows with nothing to show for it. One round with a
**mandatory evidence-backed response** is where the value is.

`ck_research_challenges_one_round` is `round_index = 1`. Allowing a second round later is a
reviewed change to a constraint, not something a caller does by passing a 2.

### The platform decides the outcome, not the responder

A response with **no evidence cannot resolve anything**, whatever it claims about itself.
A responder that could declare itself resolved would make the round decorative — and the
database agrees: a resolved challenge must carry a response, and the response must carry
evidence.

### What the round may do to a finding

| May | May not |
|---|---|
| Lower confidence | **Raise** it. A challenge that ended in a stronger claim than it started with means the responder used it as an opportunity; "this survived scrutiny" is recorded by the challenge row, not by an inflated number on the finding. |
| Withdraw the finding | Delete it. A withdrawn finding is kept with `verification_status='withdrawn'` and 7.1 already excludes it from the Council — **retiring a claim and erasing it are different things.** |

### Unresolved is an output

It becomes a `ResearchDisagreement` when a counterpart finding exists, and it reaches the
Chair through `challenges_for_chair` — **unresolved first**, because a Chair that read the
resolved ones first would be reading a defence before the objection.

When no counterpart exists, no disagreement is created. A `ResearchDisagreement` needs two
findings and the Red Team's objection is not one — it has no evidence of its own by
construction — and inventing a second finding to satisfy the schema would put an
unsupported statement in the ledger, which is the one thing it exists to prevent.

## Security considerations

Both model-facing roles are Protocols and both can fail without cost: a Red Team exception
produces zero challenges, a responder exception produces an unresolved one. A challenge
against a finding outside the run is skipped and counted — it targets a paragraph, which is
exactly what the `finding_id` exists to replace. Challenge and response text are
length-bounded.

## Unit tests

Vocabulary: an invented weakness class refused; a challenge with no stated reason refused
(an objection is not a challenge). One round: a second round unstorable **in the database**;
the index always 1; more than five challenges truncated, because a Red Team that challenges
everything has ranked nothing. The platform deciding: no evidence cannot resolve, evidence
plus a claim resolves, evidence alone is partial, an exploding responder leaves it
unresolved, an exploding Red Team does not end the run. Findings: confidence lowered,
**never raised**, and a withdrawal that keeps the row and removes it from the Council.
Unresolved: becoming a disagreement, **not** inventing a counterpart when none exists, and
reaching the Chair unresolved-first.

## Integration tests

Migration 036 against **real PostgreSQL 16**: eight statements, including that a second
round is unstorable, "resolved" with no response is unstorable, "resolved" with a response
and no evidence is unstorable, an **unresolved** challenge with no response *is* storable,
and deleting the challenged finding takes its challenges with it.

## Live issuer acceptance

Not performed. No model-backed Red Team or responder exists; both are Protocols.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A challenge targets a `finding_id`, and one naming anything outside the run is skipped.
2. An unresolved disagreement reaches the Chair intact — through the disagreement table
   when a counterpart exists, and through the challenge table always.
3. A response without evidence cannot resolve a challenge, enforced in Python **and** by
   the database.

## Non-goals

- A model-backed Red Team. The Protocol is the contract; ADR-050 notes Red Team vendor
  diversity is achievable between DeepSeek and Azure OpenAI **and no further**, and that a
  Red Team drawn from the Chair's vendor shares its blind spots.
- A second round. Deliberately unstorable.
- Chair synthesis over the challenges. The Chair consumes them; changing its output
  contract is out of scope and its vocabulary is unchanged (7.1).

## Rollback

Migration 036 downgrades cleanly — verified against real PostgreSQL. Nothing imports the
module at runtime.

## Dependencies

V3.5.1 (findings and disagreements), V3.7.1 (the Council input and the citable-id set).
